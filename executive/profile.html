01 · Positioning

Two kinds of work

How the delivery work and the research fit together.

Delivery and account ownership

I have run enterprise services end to end. That meant SLAs and escalations for a global investment bank, two international telecoms carriers and a large IT services provider. On the presales side I built solution designs and bids for a global car maker, a multinational chemicals group, a German insurer and a European bank, plus infrastructure projects for a European retail group and a global insurance broker.

That kind of work gets judged on profitability, billing volume, escalation rates and transition quality, not on how interesting the architecture is. It is where I learned to explain a technical position to the people who sign the contract.

Research and engineering

I also publish peer-reviewed work on cyber risk, systems security and database systems, and I build the systems behind it: a database engine, an XDR platform, an evidence engine for edge telemetry, and a set of shipped iOS and macOS apps.

Writing papers has made me stricter about saying what a result actually shows and what it does not.

  • 01
    Understand the problem first

    Work out which parts are requirements and which are assumptions, before quoting a solution or a price.

  • 02
    Design for how it will be run

    The people on the support rota have to be able to operate it, so their constraints belong in the design.

  • 03
    Write decisions down

    The next engineer, the customer and the auditor all end up reading it. Sprint notes are not enough.

  • 04
    Finish the handover

    A green local build is not a delivery. I check the release that customers actually get.

  • 05
    Say what is not proven

    A prototype, a tested result and a proposal are three different things, and I say which one it is.
